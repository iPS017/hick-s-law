## Typical decision points

Every link, button, menu item, chat widget, popup and banner on a landing page is an exit or a fork. On a page built for one conversion, count them all as options of the same decision: "what do I do next?".

## Rules

1. **Define the single conversion goal of the page** before anything else. Every element either supports that goal or justifies its presence.
2. **One primary CTA,** repeated with the same wording at the natural decision moments: in the hero, after the main proof section, at the end. Repeating the same action does not add a choice.
3. **Secondary actions are visually quieter.** "Book a demo" next to "Start free trial" should be a text link or a ghost button, not a twin.
4. **Paid traffic landing pages: remove or reduce the global navigation.** Keep the logo and, if needed, a link to pricing or legal pages in the footer.
5. **Match the CTA to the visitor's stage.** Cold traffic: low-commitment action (start free, see pricing). Warm traffic: direct action (buy, start plan).
6. **CTA copy names the outcome and removes a fear.** "Start your free trial" plus a microline "No card required. Cancel anytime." beats "Submit".
7. **One offer per page.** Bundling several offers (newsletter, ebook, trial, webinar) on one page splits intent.
8. **Popups, chat bubbles and banners are decisions too.** Do not open a popup before the visitor has read the hero. Do not stack a cookie banner, a chat bubble and a promo bar on first view if you can avoid it.
9. **Long pages are fine** if they answer objections. Length adds information, not decisions. Keep the CTA reachable.
10. **Social proof and FAQ near the CTA** reduce uncertainty at the moment of decision.

## When a second CTA is right

The rule is not "never more than one CTA". It is "the default next step is always obvious, and every extra CTA serves a distinct, validated intent".

- Unbounce's analysis of 18,639 landing pages found pages with one link or CTA had a higher average conversion rate (13.5%) than pages with two (11.9%) or three (10.5%). Observational benchmark, not a controlled test.
- A Tinuiti case study (Culligan) added a second path (a product finder) for visitors who were not ready to request a quote: primary CTA conversion rose 9% and overall lead conversion 17%. One case, but it shows a well-scoped second CTA can help.

Accept a second CTA when:
- It serves a different readiness level (not ready to buy: "See how it works"), and
- It leads back to the same conversion, and
- It is visually secondary.

## Audit questions

- What is the one action this page exists for? Does the hero make it obvious in five seconds?
- How many clickable exits are visible above the fold? How many have the same visual weight as the primary CTA?
- Does the CTA wording change across the page for the same action?
- Is there global navigation on a paid landing page?
- Which popups, banners or widgets appear on first view?
- Does each secondary CTA map to a distinct visitor intent?

## Test ideas

- Navigation removed vs present on paid traffic.
- Two equal buttons vs one primary plus a text link.
- Hero with one CTA vs CTA plus secondary "Watch demo".
- Popup delayed until scroll depth or exit intent vs on load.
