## Typical decision points

On a classic SaaS pricing page the visitor can face, at the same time: number of plans, billing period, seats or usage quantity, currency, add-ons, a feature comparison matrix, a free plan versus a trial, and "Contact sales". Each one is a decision. Count them all in the decision-load table.

## Rules

1. **Show 2 to 4 plans on the public page.** Keep the rest (legacy plans, niche bundles, enterprise variants) out of the grid. They can live on separate persona pages or in upgrade paths for existing customers.
2. **Recommend exactly one plan,** the one most target visitors should pick. Use a label that is true and specific ("Recommended for teams of 2 to 10") rather than a generic "Most popular" unless it is actually the most chosen plan.
3. **Name plans by who they are for or by outcome,** not by abstract tiers. The visitor should answer "is this for me?" from the plan name and one line.
4. **Lead with 3 to 5 differentiators per plan.** Put the full feature matrix below the plans, collapsed or further down. Most visitors do not need it to decide; the ones who do can open it.
5. **Make differences visible.** If two plans differ only on a limit, say it in plain numbers ("3 projects" vs "Unlimited projects"). If you cannot explain the difference in one line, merge the plans.
6. **One CTA per plan, same verb across plans,** with the recommended plan's button visually dominant.
7. **Billing period: pick a default and show the other clearly.** If annual is the default, show the monthly equivalent and the total billed, and keep the monthly option one click away. Hiding the real amount billed is drip pricing (see ethics.md).
8. **Defer add-ons.** Do not ask for add-ons on the pricing page or at plan selection. Offer them after the plan is chosen or after activation, one at a time.
9. **Free plan or trial: offer one entry path, not both,** unless they serve clearly different segments. If both exist, make one primary.
10. **Show prices.** "Contact sales" only for the enterprise tier. Hiding all prices creates a decision (do I call?) that most self-serve buyers will not take.
11. **Seats or usage:** preselect the most common value and show the computed total live. A slider or stepper beats a long dropdown.
12. **Put the reassurance next to the decision:** cancellation terms, refund policy, "no card required" for trials, FAQ about billing. These reduce uncertainty without adding choices.

## How many tiers

The evidence is mixed. Report it as such.

- Many blog posts claim large conversion gains from moving to three tiers. Most of these figures are untraceable (see evidence.md, grade D).
- One practitioner analysis of SaaS pricing data (Evan Grubb, The Tiering Dilemma) found little difference in conversion by number of tiers, while average revenue per user rose with more tiers. The author's conclusion: 2 to 4 tiers on the pricing page, extra upgrade paths for existing users.
- A 2026 census of pricing-page experiments (Lazyweb) reports that teams test both ways: adding tiers to widen the entry point and cutting tiers to reduce comparison load. Three is a common midpoint, not a rule.

Recommendation to give: keep 2 to 4 public plans, one recommended, and test the plan count against both conversion rate and revenue per visitor. Conversion alone can mislead.

## Audit questions

- How many plans are visible at once? How many have equal visual weight?
- Is there one recommended plan? Is the recommendation true?
- Can a first-time visitor say who each plan is for in five seconds?
- How many separate decisions does the page ask for before the first click (plan, period, seats, add-ons, trial vs free)?
- Is the full comparison table forced on everyone, or available on demand?
- Is the total amount billed always visible?
- Are there plans that almost nobody buys? Why are they still in the grid?

## Test ideas

- Plan count: current grid vs grid with the least chosen plan moved off the page. Watch conversion and revenue per visitor.
- Recommendation target: badge on the plan most buyers pick vs one tier up. Watch plan mix and refunds or downgrades.
- Comparison table: always visible vs collapsed behind "Compare all features".
- Billing default: annual vs monthly default, with totals always shown. Watch conversion, refunds and churn at renewal.
- Mobile plan picker: one preselected plan with "View all plans" vs full grid.
